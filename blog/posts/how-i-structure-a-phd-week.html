How I Structure a PhD Week

March 1, 2026 · 8 min read

During the PhD, progress often feels nonlinear. I use a simple week template to make sure each week contains reading, experimentation, and writing.

1) Monday: framing and planning

I start by defining one primary research objective for the week and two secondary goals. This limits context switching and protects deep work.

2) Tue–Thu: execution blocks

I reserve long uninterrupted blocks for running experiments, debugging, and reading papers connected to current experiments.

If a task takes less than 20 minutes, do it immediately. If it takes longer, schedule it intentionally.

3) Friday: synthesis

Every Friday I write a one-page weekly memo: what worked, what failed, what to try next. This keeps momentum even when results are negative.

I recommend versioning your notes the same way you version code. A plain folder with dated Markdown files already works well.
